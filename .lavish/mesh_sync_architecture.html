Secure, cloudless synchronization across 3+ trusted devices

The Zero-Trust Vault Boundary

If an unknown 4th device comes nearby, it cannot join the mesh or read data because of three strict layers of defense:

1. Public Key Whitelist

Devices reject connections from any device whose Public Key is not explicitly pre-approved.

2. Master Password

Even if they intercept the Wi-Fi signal, the AES-256 payload is mathematically impossible to read without the Argon2id key.

3. Authenticated Payloads

If they try to inject fake clipboards, the AES-GCM Authentication Tag fails and the payload is dropped.

Pairing Scenarios:

  • Phone to PC (Laptop or Desktop): The PC displays a QR code containing its Public Key. You scan it with the Android App. The Phone shares its Public Key back. They are now paired.
  • PC to PC (Laptop to 3rd Desktop): Because desktop monitors can't scan QR codes easily, the Laptop generates a temporary 6-digit numeric PIN. You type that PIN into the Desktop app. They use a standard PAKE (Password-Authenticated Key Exchange) to securely swap Public Keys.

The Mesh Gossip Sync (Background Process)

  • 1. Background Discovery

    The trusted devices constantly listen for each other's specific encrypted BLE pings. Unknown pings are ignored.

  • 2. Vector Clock Exchange

    When the Laptop and Desktop enter the same room, they connect and say: "I am at State v5. You are at State v3."

  • 3. CRDT Payload Merge

    The Laptop sends the encrypted delta of the two missing clipboard changes. The Desktop decrypts and mathematically merges them.
